How to Create a Blog Post with Claude

Go from a topic idea to a polished, publish-ready draft using a simple outline-first approach. No blank page problem.

  • ToolClaude
  • StepsSix
  • IncludesFour prompts to copy

What you'll need

Before you start
  • A topic (even a rough one is fine)
  • Who the post is for (buyers, sellers, new agents, etc.)
  • A target length (most blog posts run 600 to 900 words)
  • Any facts, listings, or local details Claude should reference

The steps

  1. Give Claude the topic, audience, and goal

    Start with context, not a request for a full draft. Tell Claude what the post is about, who is reading it, and what you want them to do after reading it.

    Example prompt

    "I need a blog post for our real estate website. Topic: what first-time buyers should know before their first showing. Audience: first-time buyers in Virginia Beach. Goal: build trust and get them to book a call with an agent."

  2. Ask for an outline before a draft

    Do not ask for the finished post yet. Ask for a heading structure first. This lets you fix the direction before Claude writes a single paragraph, which saves you from rewriting the whole thing later.

    Example prompt

    "Before writing the full post, give me an outline with a headline and 4 to 5 subheadings covering the key points."

  3. Approve or adjust the outline

    Read it like a table of contents. Reorder sections, cut ones that do not fit, or ask Claude to add one you know your audience needs. This step takes two minutes and prevents most rewrite headaches.

  4. Generate the full draft from the approved outline

    Once the outline is right, ask Claude to write the full post from it. Give a word count target and remind it of the tone you want.

    Example prompt

    "Write the full post from this outline. Aim for 700 words, friendly and clear, no jargon. Add a short intro and a closing paragraph with a call to action to contact an agent."

  5. Edit for voice and accuracy

    Read the full draft once for tone: does it sound like your brand, not a generic AI voice? Then read it again for facts: prices, dates, names, and local details. Claude cannot verify these, so you have to.

  6. Run a final tightening pass

    Paste the edited draft back in and ask Claude to tighten it: shorter sentences, no repeated ideas, a stronger closing line. This last pass is quick and usually improves readability the most.

    Example prompt

    "Tighten this draft. Shorten long sentences, remove repetition, and make the closing paragraph stronger. Keep the meaning the same."

Before you publish

Important

Always fact-check before publishing. Verify any price, statistic, date, or claim about the market. Claude does not have access to live data and can produce a confident-sounding statement that is out of date or simply wrong.
