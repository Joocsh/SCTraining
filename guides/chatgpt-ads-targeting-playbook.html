Writing Targeting Hints for ChatGPT Ads

ChatGPT Ads does not target keywords. It targets a moment, described in plain language. This walks through writing that description well, for whichever real estate moment you want to show up in.

  • Library25 context hints
  • IncludesSample headlines and descriptions

What this actually is

Inside a ChatGPT Ads campaign, each ad group has exactly two real inputs: a landing page, and a short paragraph called a context hint. There is no keyword field in the way older ad platforms use the term. The system reads your context hint alongside your ad copy and your landing page, then decides whether to show your ad next to a live conversation. The whole craft of targeting here comes down to describing the person and the moment clearly enough that the system recognizes it.

A good context hint follows a simple pattern: who the person is, where they are located, what they are trying to do, and what moment or trigger brought them to this conversation in the first place. Get those four pieces right and the rest of the ad group tends to fall into place.

The pattern to follow

Persona, location, intent, moment
  • Persona, who this person is: a first time buyer, a frustrated seller, an investor
  • Location, always local: your city, your metro, or the specific neighborhood you serve
  • Intent, what they are actually trying to do right now
  • Moment, the timing or trigger that brought them to this conversation

How to use it

Pick four to six context hints that match your business. Don't try to run all 25 — you'll fragment your spend and dilute your testing. Build a dedicated landing page for each one (or use the best fallback you have), paste the hint into the Context Hints field in your ad group, drop in the sample headlines and descriptions, and point your ad at the right URL. Test, measure, refine.

The hints below are organized into three sections: Agent Moments (someone looking for an agent), Seller Moments, and Buyer Moments. Swap your city, metro, or neighborhood into the bracketed placeholders before you paste.

Agent Moments

These describe the moment someone is searching for a person, not a property. They're at the top of the funnel for agent selection itself — the highest-intent moments in the library.

1. Buyer or seller searching for the best local agent

Use your About / bio page. Downshift to your homepage.

Context hint

People in [CITY/METRO] who are getting ready to buy or sell a home and trying to figure out which real estate agent to work with. They want to know who the top-performing agents in the area are, who has the best track record in their specific neighborhood or price range, and how to choose someone they can trust with one of the biggest financial decisions they'll make.

Headlines: How to Pick the Right [CITY] Agent  ·  [NUMBER] Years Selling Homes in [NEIGHBORHOOD]  ·  The Questions Most People Forget to Ask

Descriptions: Choosing an agent is one of the biggest decisions you'll make this year. Here's how to do it right.  ·  Same listing, very different outcomes. See how a [NEIGHBORHOOD] specialist actually works before you sign.

Context hint

Before you launch

  • Don't run all 25 at once. Pick four to six that match your business. Trying to test everything simultaneously fragments your spend and dilutes the signal.
  • Build the landing page before you launch the ad. Sending a niche-targeted ad to a generic homepage is the fastest way to waste money. The matcher reads your landing page as a signal — if it doesn't reinforce the hint, match quality drops. Every specialization page you don't have yet is a build assignment.
  • Vary your headlines and descriptions intentionally. Each one should be a different angle — a question, a specific number, an objection-disarm, a callout — not the same idea reworded. The matcher gets stronger signal from real variety.
  • Keep your image simple and on-message. OpenAI recommends clear, uncluttered visuals that match what the headline and description are saying. A friendly headshot, a clean shot of a local home, or a recognizable neighborhood landmark works. Skip stock photos of generic skylines.
  • Fair Housing matters here too. These hints and sample headlines are written to target housing situations and financial moments, not protected classes. Don't modify them to reference family status, marital status, age, religion, or anything else covered by Fair Housing. Target the housing need, not the household type.
