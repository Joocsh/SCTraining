The Art of Living.

The evolution of Sotheby’s International Realty.

MID 1970s

A recurring pattern

The Sotheby’s auction house noticed a recurring pattern: their wealthiest clients weren’t just buying Rare Dutch Masters or Ming vases; they were often buying and selling the grand estates required to house them.

1976

Launched as a subsidiary

The original intent was simple: to provide a specialized real estate service for the auction house’s existing clientele. By leveraging the trust and prestige of the Sotheby’s name, they created a seamless pipeline between the world of fine art and luxury property.

NEARLY 30 YEARS

A brokerage, and its ceiling

SIR operated primarily as a brokerage, owning its own offices. However, the brand’s global potential was limited by the capital required to open physical locations everywhere.

2004: from brokerage to franchise.

For 30 years SIR owned its own offices, which capped how fast it could grow. The 2004 agreement changed that.

The deal
Realogy paid $100 million to license the SIR name for 50 years, with options to renew.
The result
Independent luxury brokerages worldwide could rebrand as SIR: instant prestige of a 280 year old name, still locally owned.
Early 2026
Anywhere and Compass merged into Compass International Holdings. SIR keeps its distinct identity as the ultra luxury crown jewel of the portfolio.

A flexible system, not a rigid template.

Layouts are designed to reflect the era of each property and adapt to varying amounts of information. A centered, simple, classic layout appeals to our established core audiences.

Two ends of a spectrum. These are not the only approaches. You can take elements from each to create layouts that blend both.

Two rules that fix most layouts.

Image scale
Hero images should be half the canvas width or more so they stay the most prominent element. Supporting images stay significantly smaller to build hierarchy.
Spacing
Every element needs room to breathe so it stays discernible in complex layouts. This applies to text most of all.

Where the logo goes, and how much room it needs.

Positioning

Classic layouts: logo centered at the bottom. Modern layouts: logo in a corner.

Minimum distance

Clear space from any element or canvas edge equals the height of the “S”, on every edge.

Three colors. No exceptions.

Click any swatch to copy its hex code.

Why off white exists. It brings a warm, distinctive, modern background to the campaign and lets the brand stand out subtly on predominantly white spaces, like a banner on a mostly white website.

One rule that saves a reprint.

Always safe
SIR blue and pure white work in both backgrounds and elements, on any print process.
Newsprint
Use pure white for the light background. Avoid off white, it can appear muddy on the page.
Off white
High quality lithographic and digital print only.

Two families, one hierarchy.

Freight Display Pro is the primary typeface for headlines, titles and short statements. Benton Sans covers subheaders, body copy and calls to action.

Weights available. Freight Disp Pro ships Light, Book, Medium, Semibold, Bold and Black, each with an italic. Benton Sans ships Thin through Black, plus Extra Condensed, Condensed and Compressed widths.

The specimen is the deck page itself, so those are the real letterforms. Both families are licensed: pull them from the SIR asset library rather than substituting a lookalike.

Five shapes to hold every image.

Rectangle
Most functional. Holds any image at any size, usually a secondary asset or when the more elaborate shapes cannot be used.
Oval & arch
Most unique. Brilliant for portrait imagery, and they hold landscape too.
Curve, step, double step
As flexible as a rectangle but with distinctive corner cutouts. They work whole or cropped.

What turns a vague request into a great response.

1 · Role
Tell Claude who to be: expertise, tone, perspective.
2 · Context
The background. Who the audience is, what the situation is.
3 · Task
Exactly what you want done. Use action verbs.
4 · Format
Length, structure, style. Bullet list, two paragraphs, table.
5 · Example
Optional but powerful. Show what good looks like.

The same request, two results.

Claude works best when it understands who it is talking to, what the situation is, and what success looks like. Most people skip these details and then wonder why the output feels generic.

“Write me a social post for a new listing.”

Claude has no audience, no brand voice, no format. You get something generic that needs a full rewrite.

Role as a real estate social copywriter, context with the brand colors, tone and audience, task with the exact deliverable, and format with slide count and character limits.

You get something you can post after a quick review.

RoleContextTaskFormat
Act as a senior graphic designer specializing in real estate brand identity. I am attaching a post I designed for a new listing. Our brand guidelines specify: primary colors [HEX + HEX], typography [DISPLAY FONT] for headlines and [BODY FONT] for body text, a clean and aspirational tone, and logo placement [POSITION]. Review this post and tell me specifically what is aligned with our brand guidelines and what is not. For each issue found, explain why it breaks the guideline and give me a concrete fix. Structure your response with two sections: "What works" and "What needs fixing." Under each section use a short bullet list. Keep your language direct, I need to action this quickly.

Key move: Giving Claude the exact brand values (hex codes, fonts, rules) means the review is specific, not generic. The two section format forces actionable output, not a wall of commentary.

Act as a graphic designer and digital marketing strategist for a luxury real estate agency. We are launching a new residential development called "[NAME]" — a [TYPE] targeting [AUDIENCE]. Act as an expert transaction coordinator with deep knowledge of real estate documentation requirements, disclosure laws, and compliance standards. I am coordinating a residential listing in [CITY, STATE/PROVINCE]. The property is a [PROPERTY TYPE: single-family home / condo / townhouse], listed at [$PRICE], with [SELLER NAME] as the client. We are working with [BROKERAGE NAME] and the listing agent is [AGENT NAME]. Based on the specific legal and MLS requirements for this location, give me a complete master document checklist organized into three phases: (1) Pre-listing — everything needed before the property goes live on MLS, (2) Active listing — documents required while the property is on market, and (3) Under contract to close — every document, disclosure, and form needed from accepted offer through closing day.
