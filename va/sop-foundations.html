FOUNDATION

A process tells the story. An SOP makes it repeatable.

Understanding the difference is the first step toward useful documentation.

PROCESS

What happens

A process is a series of actions or steps taken to achieve a specific goal. It describes what happens and the order in which it happens.

Examples: checking and responding to client emails, onboarding a client, or preparing a weekly report.

SOP

How it happens

A Standard Operating Procedure is a detailed, written, step-by-step guide explaining exactly how to complete a process consistently and accurately.

It documents instructions, tools, responsibilities, and troubleshooting.

AspectProcessSOP
FocusWhat happens and in what orderHow to do each step correctly and consistently
FormatWorkflow diagram, checklist, or verbal instructionsDetailed documented guide
PurposeOrganizes work and shows workflowEnsures consistent, efficient, accurate execution
AudienceAnyone involved in the workflowAnyone performing it, especially new team members

Where SOPs apply

Use SOPs during onboarding, daily operations, team handoffs, and process audits—whenever a process requires consistency and clarity.

Why are team handoffs a strong use case?

SOPs provide a common reference for coordination, smooth transitions, and accountability when work moves between team members.

OFFICIAL SOP TEMPLATE

The structure your SOP must follow.

The official template organizes documentation so another team member knows the purpose, timing, ownership, steps, resources, and exceptions.

  1. PurposeWHY — purpose / objective: the goal of the process and what it is trying to accomplish.
  2. ScopeWHEN — timing, frequency, or triggers; when the SOP needs to be used.
  3. DefinitionsExplain terms needed to understand the SOP.
  4. Roles and ResponsibilitiesWHO — who performs each part, and who reviews or approves.
  5. ProcedureHOW — clear, ordered step-by-step instructions.
  6. Documented InformationWITH WHAT — tools, platforms, templates, or resources used to complete the process.
  7. 6.1 ReferencesDocument/letter, form, and platform.
  8. 6.2 TroubleshootingWHAT IF — exceptions, errors, support, and what to do when something goes wrong.
  9. 6.3 Related SOPsRelated code and documentation name.
  10. FlowchartA visual representation of the process.
  11. Version ControlVersion, date modified, description, and author.
Template reminder: The template is more detailed than a simple title-and-steps checklist. Keep every relevant section, including references, troubleshooting, related SOPs, flowchart, and version control.
QUALITY WRITING

Write so a new teammate can succeed without guessing.

Strong SOP habits

  • Start with the end in mind: know why the SOP exists.
  • Use simple, direct language, short sentences, and action verbs.
  • Be specific and detailed without overwhelming the reader.
  • Use numbered steps and bullets for scanability.
  • Add screenshots, links, or visual aids where useful.
  • Use a consistent format across SOPs.
  • Review and test the SOP; ask someone else to follow it.
  • Review periodically—every 6–12 months or after a system/tool update.

Make instructions actionable

Less direct:

“It is required that the report be submitted by the VA.”

Clearer:

“Send the report to the team lead.”

Use descriptive titles and store SOPs in an accessible, organized location.

INSTRUCTIONAL SCENARIO

Spot the documentation gap

An SOP lists “Open the tracker and update it,” but provides no tracker link, name, or platform. What should be strengthened?

Documented Information (WITH WHAT). Identify the tool, platform, template, or resource needed so the associate can complete the process consistently.

OFFICIAL ASSESSMENT GUIDE

Your SOP assessment: follow the required order.

1

Review the VA SOP Handbook

Begin by carefully reading it. It contains the process explanation, context, rules, expectations, roles, tools, timing, and exceptions. Quiz answers and SOP content must be based exclusively on the handbook.

2

Complete the knowledge quiz

This confirms comprehension. The assessment is not a memorization exercise; it evaluates process understanding, logical thinking, attention to detail, and standardized documentation.

3

Create the Executive Assistant SOP

Copy the official SOP Template and use that copy to build the “Executive Assistant” SOP from the Executive Assistant Handbook. Reflect the handbook completely, use the official structure, write clearly and professionally, and add no assumptions or external information.

4

Export and submit

Export the completed SOP as a PDF. Email it to ea.coaching@skillcloudstaffing.com with the subject line SOP Assessment – [Your Name]. Coaches review it and provide feedback.

Evaluation criteria

  • Accuracy and alignment with the handbook
  • Completeness—no missing sections
  • Clarity and ease of following
  • Proper use of the SOP template
  • Attention to detail—no invented steps or tools
  • Professional writing and formatting

Key rules

  • Do not copy text blindly; understand and rephrase appropriately.
  • Do not add steps, tools, or rules not in the handbook.
  • Use clear, professional English.
  • Follow the SOP structure exactly as provided.

Keep the six questions close.

Before you create an SOP

  1. Read and understand the authoritative handbook or process source.
  2. Use the official template and complete its relevant sections.
  3. Write steps a new team member can follow without guessing.
  4. Test, review, maintain, and update the SOP as processes change.
