Foundations

What an AI tool is

Know what a confident answer does, and does not, tell you.

1

A plain-English definition

An AI tool is software that uses learned patterns to help recognize, generate, transform, or organize information. A language model generates text by estimating likely next pieces of text from the instructions and context. This can produce useful writing without proving that its claims are true. Not every AI system is a language model.

2

What it can help with

Language tools can follow written instructions, draft messages, summarize reports, categorize notes, and organize lists. The quality depends on the task, available information, model, and instructions. A summary can omit a condition; a polished email can invent a promise.

3

What confidence does not prove

A hallucination is an output that invents or misstates information. Fluent wording is not evidence. Verify names, dates, numbers, property features, and sources against authoritative records. If the tool cannot establish a fact, ask it to mark the detail as unknown.

Example

Fictional scenario: a listing sheet says “roof age unknown.” An AI description says “new roof.” Remove the claim and ask the agent for verified information.

Key takeaway

Treat generated content as a draft, including when it sounds certain.

Types of AI used in business

Match the method to the task before choosing a vendor.

1

Generative AI

Creates or transforms content from instructions: an email draft, SOP outline, social caption, or summary. Similar prompts can produce different results. Creativity is useful for alternatives, but consistency must be managed through templates, constraints, and review. “Creative AI” describes generative work with images or video; it is not a guarantee of factual accuracy.

2

Search-augmented AI

Combines retrieval with generation. It can locate information and summarize it with source links. A link is a route to verification, not proof that the summary is correct. Check the underlying source, publication date, scope, and whether it supports the claim.

3

Embedded AI

AI functions inside software already used for work, such as an email editor or CRM. An email tone suggestion or in-app meeting summary is embedded assistance. Placement inside a familiar product does not remove privacy and review requirements.

4

Rules plus AI

Automation executes configured rules, such as creating a task when a form arrives. An optional AI step might classify the inquiry or draft a response. For the same inputs and rule state, deterministic rules aim for consistent actions; generated language may vary. Use rules for routing and moving records, and AI for analysis or drafting. These categories overlap: a CRM may embed generative AI in an automated workflow.

Example

Form submitted → validate required fields → optional AI draft → create review task → human approves message. The trigger is automation; the draft is generated content.

Key takeaway

Choose a mechanism based on the output and risk, not the logo.

Myths, capabilities, and risks

Replace assumptions with useful professional habits.

1

“AI will replace my job”

AI can automate or change tasks and roles; nobody can promise how a job will evolve. Develop the skills to direct, evaluate, and integrate it. Coordination, judgment, and human relationships still need accountable people.

2

“AI is always correct” and “AI knows everything”

Generated answers can be inaccurate or outdated. A tool does not automatically know private CRM records or your client’s current policy. Supply relevant, permitted context and verify factual claims rather than treating confident language as evidence.

3

“AI is too technical”

Many tools accept ordinary language. Clear goals, context, constraints, and a requested format matter more than technical vocabulary for everyday prompting. Complex integrations still need testing and appropriate support.

4

“Everything pasted into AI is private”

Privacy depends on the actual service, account, settings, contracts, retention, and company approval. A business label alone is not authorization for all data. Minimize inputs and ask the supervisor which environment is approved before using sensitive material.

Example

A colleague says “It is in our email app, so uploading a full client file must be safe.” Check the specific feature and approved policy before sharing any file.

Key takeaway

Convert assumptions into checks you can demonstrate.

Prompt-writing fundamentals and RACE

Give the tool the information it needs to produce a useful draft.

1

Role and Action

Role defines a useful perspective, such as “real estate operations assistant.” It does not give the tool a professional license. Action states the task: “Draft a follow-up email.” Keep the main deliverable clear and break complex work into stages.

2

Context and Expectation

Context supplies permitted facts, audience, purpose, and constraints. Expectation defines tone, length, structure, and acceptance conditions. Include “use only these facts; mark missing information” when accuracy matters. Do not include confidential material just because the prompt would be more detailed.

3

Refine deliberately

Review the first draft. Ask for a warmer tone, fewer than 100 words, simpler language, bullet points, or more focus on a verified feature. Restate important context when starting a new conversation. Clear instructions improve usefulness but cannot guarantee truth.

Example

Role: real estate assistant. Action: draft a follow-up. Context: an anonymized visitor liked the kitchen and backyard at the fictional Cedar Lane open house. Expectation: warm, under 120 words, invite a call, no invented details.

Key takeaway

RACE gives structure; verification makes the draft ready for work.

Common prompting mistakes

Fix the missing instruction instead of blaming every weak output on the tool.

1

Vague requests and missing audience

“Help with marketing” leaves the deliverable undefined. Specify the channel, purpose, audience, and facts. “Draft an announcement email to opted-in buyers who requested listing updates” gives an operational purpose without inventing personal details.

2

Too many tasks

Researching a neighborhood, creating a campaign, and interpreting a contract in one request obscures quality and scope. Separate research from verified drafting. Send interpretation requests to a licensed professional.

3

Missing format and assumed memory

Ask for the structure you need: a table of task, owner, deadline, and dependency; a short email; or three bullets. Restate essential instructions even if they appeared earlier. Context can be missing or no longer available.

Example

Weak: “Sort this out.” Improved: “Convert these anonymized meeting notes into a table of task, owner, due date, and missing information. Do not invent deadlines.”

Key takeaway

Repair the specific missing component, then review the result.

Build a reusable prompt library

Save reliable starting points without storing confidential client context.

1

Make reuse intentional

A prompt library stores tested instructions that teammates can find, reuse, and improve. Replace client details with placeholders. Recheck facts and policy each time; a saved prompt is not a permanent guarantee of quality.

2

Categories and names

Use Email communication, Marketing, Documentation, Research, or Workflow support. A useful name is “Email, Open-house follow-up, v1.” Increment the version when changing instructions, and record the reason, owner, and review date in the team library.

3

Storage and access

A shared document, categorized folder, Notion database, or internal knowledge base may work if approved. Give team members appropriate access and remove outdated versions from the default view. This exercise’s library is only on this device, not a shared team service.

Daily workflow applications

Move from rough notes to useful communication and documentation.

1

Communication and organization

Provide permitted bullet notes, define the recipient and tone, then refine. Review the relationship context before sending. Turn meeting notes into action lists, SOP drafts, or templates with explicit categories, priorities, owners, deadlines, and unknowns.

2

Marketing support

Generate caption variations, listing drafts, subject lines, and newsletter outlines using verified facts. Review property accuracy, brand voice, and fair-housing issues. An engaging sentence is not permission to invent a feature or a demand claim.

3

Retrieval, drafting, refinement

Retrieval finds evidence and citations. Drafting structures material into the requested output. Refinement improves tone or clarity. One tool may support several steps, but each step has a different review standard. A polished summary does not replace checking its sources.

4

Source evaluation

Prefer the authority closest to the claim: a city for a city schedule, an official statistics agency for its data, an MLS or industry organization for a defined market dataset, a university for its research, and established news for reported events. Check dates, methods, geography, definitions, conflicts, and primary evidence. Anonymous blogs, unclear ownership, SEO content farms, and uncited claims require corroboration. Institutional branding alone does not prove every claim.

Example

Fictional notes: “Photos Wednesday; ask marketing; agent to confirm measurements; launch date unknown.” Request an action table that preserves the unknown launch date and assigns the measurement check to the agent.

Key takeaway

Use the original source to verify facts and the relationship context to verify tone.

Responsible AI use

Protect people and keep review mandatory, even when work is urgent.

1

Minimize and protect

Remove full names with contact details, account identifiers, financial records, legal clauses, private correspondence, and negotiation strategy from public prompts. Replace them with general placeholders and remove unnecessary context. Replacing a name alone may not anonymize a distinctive transaction. Never enter passwords or access codes into prompts.

2

Four different boundaries

Safe after anonymization: ordinary notes stripped of identifying and sensitive detail. Approved secure environment required: necessary confidential material, only with explicit company/client authorization and verified handling. Never in a public prompt: credentials, banking details, full private contracts, or identifying client records. Professional judgment: interpreting clauses, determining compliance, or making licensed decisions must be escalated regardless of the tool’s security.

3

Business accounts and policy

Use the approved account and feature, not a personal substitute. Confirm administrator settings, permitted data, retention, access, and client authorization through policy. Privacy terms differ across products and plans. This completes the handbook’s unfinished business-account subsection without assuming that a business account is automatically safe.

4

Verify, then approve

Check factual accuracy, dates, tone, relationship context, property claims, fair-housing concerns, and permission. Open cited sources. Escalate uncertainty to a supervisor, broker, or licensed professional. Keep reusable prompt records and report errors. Urgency never removes the review gate.

5

Human-in-the-loop

AI creates a draft → associate checks accuracy and completeness → responsible agent or professional adds judgment and approves → client receives the approved output. This is the review sequence illustrated in the source handbook. Approval must happen before delivery.

Example

A manager asks for a summary of a full purchase agreement in a public tool. Do not paste it. Explain the data boundary, use an approved secure process if authorized, and route interpretation to the broker.

Key takeaway

Secure handling and professional authority are separate requirements.

Choose tools by task

Evaluate the actual feature, environment, and evidence you need.

1

Task-aware selection

Begin with the objective, need for current information or citations, sensitivity of the input, output format, approved accounts, and workspace integration. A familiar tool may be suitable for drafting but not approved for confidential data. More than one tool can be acceptable.

2

Illustrative tools

Gemini supports generative assistance, with some Workspace integrations and protections depending on license and configuration. ChatGPT supports drafting and refinement and offers search with sources when that feature is used. Perplexity combines search and generated answers with source links. These are overlapping capabilities, not exclusive jobs or permanent rankings.

3

Verify before adopting

Confirm current features, privacy terms, limits, settings, and company approval with official documentation. The source handbook’s “Gemini drafts, ChatGPT polishes, Perplexity researches” is a useful workflow example, not a restriction or a best-tool claim. No tool eliminates source review.

Intermediate applications

Task and workflow management

Make every task actionable with an owner, timing, and dependencies.

1

Listing preparation

Collect approved property facts; schedule photography; draft the description; confirm measurements and features; prepare photos and channel assets; obtain agent approval; then launch and announce. Assign a role and timing for every task. Photo editing precedes upload, and final verification precedes publication.

2

Contract to close

Typical coordination stages are accepted offer, inspection, financing, pre-closing, and closing. Create the file and confirm signed records, track inspection delivery and appraisal, monitor approved milestones, confirm logistics, and archive the completed file under policy. Exact stages and responsibilities vary.

3

Pipeline and reminders

For each deal, record current status, verified next deadline, owner, blocker, and next action. A near deadline with a missing document takes priority over a routine update. Reminders might occur seven days, three days, and one day before a verified milestone, adjusted to actual policy. Never use these example intervals to calculate legal deadlines.

4

Putting it into practice

AI can draft the checklist; Asana, Monday.com, ClickUp, or Smartsheet are illustrative task platforms where approved tasks may live. Confirm integrations and permissions before use. A proposed deadline is not an agreed deadline until the responsible person confirms it.

AI-assisted transaction management

Use AI for administration while the official record and professional review stay protected.

1

Administrative scope

Organize notes, maintain checklists, draft internal status updates, and prepare files for broker review. AI does not determine legal meaning, compliance, or whether a transaction is ready to close. Use approved systems such as the team’s transaction platform for the official record. DocuSign, SkySlope, and Dotloop are examples from the handbook, not endorsements.

2

Documents and typical stages

Purchase agreement: buyer/seller, accepted offer. Property disclosures: seller, early transaction. Inspection report: inspector, inspection period. Loan documentation: buyer/lender, financing. Appraisal report: lender/appraiser workflow, financing. Closing Disclosure: lender or settlement agent according to the transaction, pre-closing. Final settlement statement: settlement/title professional, closing. The exact documents, responsibilities, and deadlines vary by jurisdiction, brokerage, contract, and transaction.

3

Prepare review, do not perform it

A broker summary includes a transaction overview, collected documents, missing documents, upcoming verified deadlines, owners, and escalation requests. Distinguish received from reviewed and approved. Track versions and official locations without copying confidential content into public tools.

4

Escalation boundary

If asked what a clause means or whether a missing document is legally acceptable, pause that decision. State the observed administrative fact and route the question securely to the responsible agent, broker, or qualified professional.

Marketing and content creation

Adapt the voice and channel while preserving verified facts.

1

One fact set, several formats

Create an MLS draft with a professional factual tone, an Instagram caption with a concise conversational tone, and a luxury-style version with restrained language. These are editorial choices, not permission to add amenities, condition, demand, or neighborhood claims. Keep a fact sheet beside every draft.

2

Brand voice

Supply two or three permitted examples, explicit tone guidance, and “avoid” rules. For example: calm and practical, short sentences, no hype, no exclamation marks. Match structure and vocabulary rather than copying private correspondence. Save the brief with a versioned prompt.

3

Email and social content

An email needs a subject line, short introduction, verified highlights, and a clear call to action. Social versions can change length, hook, and tone while retaining the same facts. Review consent and company requirements before scheduling. Do not invent “offers arriving now” or other urgency.

4

Property facts and fairness

Flag language about who should live in a home, such as “perfect for families,” for professional review and neutral revision. “Top-rated schools” is not automatically a verified fact. School-quality information can be shared objectively and consistently under current policy; check the source and avoid steering. Focus advertisements on property features and neutral amenities. Brokerage, state, and local requirements also apply.

Advanced systems

AI for sales communication

Capture useful facts and follow up with value and permission.

1

Consent before capture

Before recording or transcribing, confirm jurisdiction-specific and context-specific requirements through current brokerage policy and qualified guidance. Follow the approved consent process. If authorization is unclear, do not enable recording. This course intentionally avoids state-by-state legal shortcuts.

2

Structured notes

Extract Client objective, Property preferences, Timeline, Budget, Concerns, and Next actions. Distinguish stated facts from guesses. Mark absent information as not stated. Have the agent confirm the summary before entering approved notes into the CRM. Minimize and protect recordings and transcripts.

3

Value-based follow-up

A useful sequence responds to the actual inquiry: offer relevant verified information, provide it, ask a focused question, offer a concrete next step, and respect pause or opt-out signals. Confirm messaging permission, channel rules, and policy before launch. Stop or route a sequence when the lead responds, opts out, or needs human help.

Lead scoring and pipeline tracking

Prioritize service using observed, job-relevant signals.

1

Transparent prioritization

Use stated timeline, recent engagement, confirmed readiness, and explicit requests to plan follow-up. Document the reason, next action, owner, and review date. A priority label supports scheduling; it does not decide eligibility or deny service. Humans remain responsible.

2

Avoid bias

Do not use protected characteristics or proxies for them to rank people or restrict service. Do not infer readiness from family status, ethnicity, age, disability, or neighborhood stereotypes. A simple visible rubric helps expose inappropriate inputs, but humans must still audit outcomes and context.

3

Observed versus inferred

“Pre-approval may be expired” does not mean renewal is easy. “Another agent was involved” means the responsible agent should clarify representation; it does not prove the relationship ended. Ask for missing information rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

4

Data quality

Duplicate records, stale timestamps, inconsistent stages, and missing consent can make a score misleading. Clean the inputs and record unknowns. A low score caused by incomplete data should trigger clarification, not automatic neglect.

Turn patterns into testable improvements without overstating what the data proves.

1

What to observe

First-response time, follow-up consistency, message specificity, conversion, and drop-off can show where to investigate. Define each measure before comparing. Missing timestamps, duplicate leads, different lead sources, or unequal observation windows can distort the picture.

2

Patterns are not causes

If faster responses are associated with more showings, that does not establish that speed alone caused the difference. Lead intent, staffing, source, and timing may differ. Small samples are especially uncertain. State the period, denominator, missing data, and alternative explanations.

3

From observation to action

Automation and system integration

Design the approval and failure paths alongside the happy path.

1

Connect work deliberately

Lead forms, CRM records, communication tools, transaction systems, marketing platforms, and task boards can exchange information through approved integrations. Zapier and Make are illustrative automation platforms. Confirm connectors, permissions, data handling, and current capabilities before implementation.

2

Lead intake

Trigger: form submission. Conditions: required fields, consent, valid routing, and duplicate check. AI step: optional inquiry classification or draft from permitted inputs. Actions: create/update a record and assign a task. Human approval: review generated content before client use. Log/error path: record outcomes and notify an owner when a step fails.

3

Tasks and behavior triggers

A verified new-listing stage can create photography, drafting, fact-checking, and launch tasks. A showing booking can produce an approved confirmation; a completed showing can queue a feedback request. Timings are configured policy choices, not universal legal standards. Pause sequences after an opt-out, response, or escalation.

4

Safeguards

Use least-privilege permissions, stable record IDs, duplicate prevention, bounded retries, alerts, and an audit trail. An idempotent action avoids creating another record when the same event is retried. If AI returns uncertain or invalid output, send it to a human queue. Test missing fields, repeat events, permission failures, and outages before enabling a workflow.

Reporting dashboards

Give management a clear measure, a limitation, and a practical next action.

1

Metric definitions

New leads: distinct records created in the period. Average first-response time: elapsed minutes for records with a valid response timestamp; show missing responses separately. Showings scheduled: count of leads with a scheduled showing under the chosen definition. Lead-to-showing conversion: showing leads divided by all leads in the selected cohort. Days from first contact to contract: duration for contracted records with both dates.

2

Interpret carefully

New lead volume describes intake, not quality. Response time describes speed, not satisfaction or causation. Showings indicate activity, not completed sales. Conversion depends on the cohort and observation window. Contact-to-contract duration excludes records that have not reached contract and can therefore be biased.

3

Management report structure

Include lead generation, agent activity, conversion metrics, operational observations, and recommended actions. State the period and missing data. Assign owners to proposed changes. Prefer “review unanswered leads” to “AI proves this agent is underperforming.”

4

Reporting workflow

Pull only approved fields from authoritative systems, validate duplicates and timestamps, aggregate, review, then publish through the approved channel. Keep individual client details out of broad reports when aggregates are enough.
