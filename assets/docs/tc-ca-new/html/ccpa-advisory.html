2. Notice of Data Collection

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), grants California residents certain rights regarding their personal information. In connection with the listing and sale of the above-referenced property, personal information about the Seller(s) will be collected, used, and shared as necessary to complete the real estate transaction.

This advisory is provided to inform Seller(s) about the types of personal information collected, the purposes for which it is used, and the third parties with whom it may be shared.

During the course of this real estate transaction, the following categories of personal information may be collected from the Seller(s):

Category Examples

Government-Issued IDs Social Security numbers (for IRS 1099-S reporting and FIRPTA compliance), driver's license numbers (for notarization and identity verification)

4. Purpose of Data Collection

Personal information is collected and used for the following purposes:

Marketing and advertising the property for sale (MLS listing, internet syndication, print materials)
Preparing and executing listing and transaction documents
Facilitating the escrow and title process
Complying with federal, state, and local tax reporting requirements
Complying with anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) regulations
Processing the transfer of property ownership and funds

6. Seller Rights Under the CCPA

As California residents, Seller(s) have the following rights under the CCPA/CPRA:

Right to Know: Seller(s) may request disclosure of the categories and specific pieces of personal information collected.

Right to Delete: Seller(s) may request deletion of personal information, subject to legal exceptions (e.g., information required for tax reporting or legal compliance cannot be deleted).

Right to Correct: Seller(s) may request correction of inaccurate personal information.

Right to Opt Out of Sale/Sharing: Seller(s) may opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information. Note that sharing information necessary to complete the real estate transaction is not considered a "sale" under the CCPA.

Right to Non-Discrimination: Seller(s) will not be discriminated against for exercising their CCPA rights.

7. Data Retention

Retention Period:
Transaction records will be retained for a minimum of 5 years from the close of escrow, as required by California Bureau of Real Estate (CalBRE) regulations (10 Cal. Code Regs. §2724).
Tax Records:
Documents related to tax reporting (1099-S, settlement statements) may be retained for up to 7 years in accordance with IRS record-keeping requirements.
Post-Retention:
After the applicable retention period, personal information will be securely destroyed or de-identified.
