What Is Dual Agency?

Dual agency occurs when the same brokerage firm (or the same individual agent) represents both the seller and the buyer in the same transaction. Under California Civil Code Section 2079.17, dual agency is permitted only with the informed written consent of both the seller and the buyer.

In a dual agency situation, the agent(s) and brokerage owe fiduciary duties to both parties. However, a dual agent may not, without the express written permission of the respective party:

Disclose to the Buyer that the Seller is willing to accept a price less than the listing price.
Disclose to the Seller that the Buyer is willing to pay a price greater than the offering price.
Disclose confidential motivations, urgency, or personal information of one party to the other.
Favor one party's interest over the other in negotiations or advice.

3. Potential Conflicts of Interest

Seller(s) should be aware of the following potential conflicts that may arise in a dual agency situation:

Reduced Advocacy: In a dual agency, the agent cannot advocate exclusively for the Seller as they would in a single-agency relationship. The agent must treat both parties fairly and honestly.

Information Limitations: The dual agent cannot share strategic information, pricing motivations, or negotiating positions between the parties without written consent.

Brokerage-Level Dual Agency: Even if different individual agents within the brokerage represent the seller and the buyer, the brokerage itself is a dual agent and must comply with dual agency requirements.
