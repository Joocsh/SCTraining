2. General Advisory

This advisory provides Seller(s) with a general overview of the real estate selling process in California. It is intended as informational guidance only and does not constitute legal, tax, or financial advice. Seller(s) are encouraged to consult with appropriate professionals regarding their specific circumstances.

Important: Real estate transactions involve complex legal documents, significant financial decisions, and strict contractual timelines. Seller(s) should carefully review all documents before signing and promptly communicate any questions or concerns to their Listing Agent.

3. Seller Disclosure Obligations

Under California law, Seller(s) have a duty to disclose all known material facts about the property that could affect its value or desirability. Key disclosure requirements include:

Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS): Disclose known defects, repairs, and property conditions.
Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ): Provide supplemental information about the property's history.
Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD): Third-party report on natural hazard zones.
Lead-Based Paint Disclosure: Required for pre-1978 homes (this property built 1961).
Smoke Detector / CO Detector Compliance: Must be in compliance at close of escrow.
Water Heater Bracing: Statement of compliance required.

Failure to disclose known material facts can result in legal liability after the sale. When in doubt, disclose.

4. Escrow Process Timeline

Once an offer is accepted, the transaction typically proceeds through the following phases:

Phase Typical Timeframe Key Activities
Opening Escrow Days 1–3 Earnest money deposit, escrow instructions, title order
Inspections & Disclosures Days 1–17 Buyer inspections, seller disclosures delivered, NHD report
Appraisal Days 10–21 Lender-ordered appraisal, appraisal review
Loan Approval Days 17–25 Buyer's loan conditions cleared, final underwriting
Contingency Removals Days 17–21 Buyer removes inspection, appraisal, and loan contingencies
Closing & Recording Days 25–30 Final walkthrough, signing, funding, deed recording

Note: Timelines are approximate and may vary based on terms negotiated in the purchase agreement.

5. Property Condition Responsibilities

Seller(s) should maintain the property in its current condition through the close of escrow. Seller(s) are responsible for:

Maintaining all systems, appliances, and fixtures in working order.
Keeping landscaping and grounds reasonably maintained.
Not making material alterations without buyer's written consent.
Providing access for inspections and appraisal as scheduled.

6. Repairs and Credits

Following buyer inspections, the buyer may submit a Request for Repair (C.A.R. Form RR). Seller(s) should be aware:

a) Seller is not obligated to agree to all repair requests. Repairs are negotiable between the parties.

b) Seller may offer to make repairs, provide a credit toward buyer's closing costs, or reduce the purchase price in lieu of repairs.

c) All repairs performed by Seller should be completed by licensed and insured contractors, with receipts and permits provided to the buyer.

d) Seller should not attempt to conceal or cosmetically cover up known defects.

8. Tax Implications

The sale of real property may trigger tax obligations. Seller(s) should consult with a tax professional regarding:

Capital Gains Tax: If the property has appreciated in value, Seller(s) may owe federal and/or California capital gains tax on the profit. Under current IRS rules, individuals may exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly) if the property was used as a primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years.

1099-S Reporting: The escrow company will issue IRS Form 1099-S to report the gross proceeds from the sale. This form is filed with the IRS and must be reported on Seller's tax return.

California Withholding: California may require withholding of 3.33% of the gross sale price unless an exemption applies (e.g., the property is a primary residence and the sale price is under $750,000, or the gain does not exceed the capital gains exclusion).
