2. Federal Fair Housing Act

The Federal Fair Housing Act (Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, as amended) prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing based on the following protected classes:

Race
Color
Religion
National Origin
Sex (including gender identity and sexual orientation)
Familial Status (families with children under 18)
Disability (physical or mental)

3. California Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA)

California provides additional fair housing protections under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (Government Code §12955 et seq.). In addition to the federal protected classes, California law also prohibits discrimination based on:

Marital Status
Ancestry
Source of Income
Sexual Orientation
Gender Identity / Gender Expression
Genetic Information
Primary Language
Immigration Status
Military or Veteran Status
Age

4. Prohibited Conduct in Real Estate

Under both federal and California law, the following actions are prohibited when based on a protected class:

a) Refusing to sell, rent, or negotiate for the sale or rental of a dwelling.

b) Discriminating in the terms, conditions, or privileges of a sale or rental.

c) Making, printing, or publishing any statement or advertisement that indicates a preference, limitation, or discrimination.

d) Representing that a dwelling is not available for inspection, sale, or rental when it is in fact available.

e) Steering or channeling prospective buyers or renters toward or away from particular neighborhoods.

f) Blockbusting — profiting by inducing owners to sell by representing that persons of a particular protected class are moving into the neighborhood.

g) Denying or discriminating in the provision of real estate brokerage services.
