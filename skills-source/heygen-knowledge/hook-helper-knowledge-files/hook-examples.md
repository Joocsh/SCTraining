# Listing Video Hook Examples

Reference patterns for creative hook concepts. These are templates to adapt to specific properties — not scripts to copy verbatim. Each hook should be customized to the listing's unique features, price point, and neighborhood.

## Hook Pattern Library

### 1. The Hot Listing

**Concept:** The agent approaches the property and discovers it's literally "hot" — touches the door and reacts as if it's burning. Visual metaphor for a hot listing.

**Visual:** Walk-up to front door, palm on door, pull back sharply, turn to camera with knowing grin.

**Script sketch:** "Yep. Just what I thought. This listing is hot. You've gotta see the inside."

**Best mode:** Cinematic (requires movement, door interaction, environment)

**Best for:** Well-priced listings, new-to-market properties, competitive neighborhoods

**Transition to tour:** Cut on "the inside" — hard cut to interior walkthrough footage.

---

### 2. The Vault

**Concept:** Warm golden light pours out of the home's windows and door as if treasure is inside. Visual metaphor for equity or value.

**Visual:** Agent stands in front of home with golden glow illuminating their face. Raised eyebrows, knowing grin, gestures toward the glowing door.

**Script sketch:** "There's $[X] in equity behind that door. Want to see what it looks like inside?"

**Best mode:** Cinematic (requires lighting effects, environment, gesture toward house)

**Best for:** High-equity properties, underpriced listings, investment opportunities

**Transition to tour:** Cut from glowing exterior to actual interior first shot.

---

### 3. The Walk-Up

**Concept:** Agent walks toward camera confidently, stops, points directly at viewer. Direct, urgent, breaks the fourth wall.

**Visual:** Confident stride toward camera in front of property. Stops, locks eyes on camera, points.

**Script sketch:** "Stop scrolling. This one just hit the market and it won't last the weekend."

**Best mode:** Cinematic (requires locomotion, environment)

**Best for:** New listings, fast-moving markets, urgency-driven hooks

**Transition to tour:** Agent's gesture or head turn cues the cut to property footage.

---

### 4. The Reveal

**Concept:** Agent stands beside the front door, hand on handle, opens it. Warm light spills out. The door-opening IS the transition into the tour.

**Visual:** Hand resting on door, excited look to camera, pushes door open, warm interior light.

**Script sketch:** "Three beds, two baths, and a price that's going to surprise you. Ready?"

**Best mode:** Cinematic (requires door interaction, lighting change, movement)

**Best for:** Properties with impressive interiors, wow-factor entries, open floor plans

**Transition to tour:** The door opening is the literal transition — cut to interior as door swings.

---

### 5. The Hot Take

**Concept:** Pattern interrupt — agent holds a serious expression and a beat of silence, then breaks into a smile. The tonal shift hooks the viewer.

**Visual:** Serious face, holding a beat. Then slow smile breaks across face. Slight lean-in.

**Script sketch:** "Your landlord doesn't want you to see this listing." OR "This townhouse costs less per month than most two-bedroom apartments. I'll prove it."

**Best mode:** Presenter (performance-driven, no environment needed) OR Cinematic

**Best for:** Rent-vs-buy angle, controversial takes, market commentary hooks

**Transition to tour:** The "I'll prove it" or "let me show you" line bridges to property footage.

---

### 6. The Numbers Drop

**Concept:** Agent counts on fingers while rattling off the listing's key stats. Fast-paced, data-as-drama format.

**Visual:** Confident posture, arms crossed then uncrossed. Counts on fingers as they speak. Energetic.

**Script sketch:** "Three bedrooms. Two and a half baths. One price you won't believe. Let me walk you through it."

**Best mode:** Presenter (counting on fingers is a gesture Avatar V handles well) OR Cinematic

**Best for:** Well-spec'd listings, value-proposition properties, competitive price points

**Transition to tour:** "Let me walk you through it" bridges directly to tour footage.

---

### 7. The Neighborhood Insider

**Concept:** Agent appears in front of a recognizable neighborhood landmark or streetscape. Positions themselves as the local expert.

**Visual:** Casual, relaxed posture in front of the neighborhood. Warm smile, open gesture toward surroundings.

**Script sketch:** "I've sold [X] homes in [neighborhood] this year. This one might be my favorite. Here's why."

**Best mode:** Cinematic (requires environment immersion)

**Best for:** Neighborhood-centric marketing, hyperlocal agents, community-focused branding

**Transition to tour:** "Here's why" cues the cut to property exterior or interior.

---

### 8. The Before/After Tease

**Concept:** Agent hints at a transformation — renovation, staging, or market value shift. Creates curiosity gap.

**Visual:** Knowing expression, slight head shake as if impressed, gestures as if weighing two options.

**Script sketch:** "They bought this for $[X] two years ago. Wait until you see what it's worth now."

**Best mode:** Presenter (performance-driven, curiosity is in the delivery)

**Best for:** Flips, renovations, appreciation stories, investment case studies

**Transition to tour:** Cut to property footage after the value reveal.

---

## Adapting Hooks to Properties

When brainstorming hooks for a specific listing, consider:

1. **What's the one thing that makes this listing stand out?** The hook should dramatize THAT thing.
2. **Who's the buyer?** First-time buyer hooks differ from luxury buyer hooks differ from investor hooks.
3. **What's the market context?** A hot market makes urgency hooks authentic. A slow market makes value hooks more credible.
4. **What platform is it for?** Reels/Shorts demand faster pacing and more visual impact. YouTube allows slightly longer setups.
5. **What's the agent's brand?** Playful agents can do visual gags. Luxury agents need polished, cinematic energy. Data-driven agents can lead with numbers.

## Hooks to Avoid

- **Anything requiring complex VFX** (explosions, magic, flying objects) — AI video can't reliably render these
- **Hooks that require precise text rendering** on objects in the scene — AI video struggles with readable text
- **Hooks longer than 8 seconds** — the hook should create intrigue, not tell the whole story
- **Hooks that mislead about the property** — Fair Housing and MLS compliance still apply to AI-generated content
- **Hooks that require the avatar to interact with tiny props** (keys, phones, pens) — hand artifacts are common
